# The simulator

`/simulator` shows the books moving. Each container of a scene is drawn as a tank with depth (a rim and a liquid level). The accounts sit inside it as stacked bands. When a transaction is played, coins fly from where value comes from to where it goes, and the bands grow or shrink. The equation line under the tanks says whether the two sides agree. Every step is also given in words, and those words are announced to a screen reader.

The first scene is the accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Capital. A Suspense tank appears whenever an error leaves the books one-sided. Incomes and expenses flow into the capital tank as the "profit so far", and drawings flow out of it.

## Modes

- **Watch.** Pick a transaction (the starter list, or the endless accounting-equation and journal-entry patterns) and see it play step by step. Then read what happened, why each side moved, the journal entry and the ledger accounts. You can play, pause, step, replay and set the speed (0.5x, 1x, 2x). From the keyboard, Space plays or pauses, the right arrow plays one step and R replays.
- **Predict.** Before a transaction plays, say whether each side goes up, goes down or stays the same. At the higher levels, also give the amount, and then the accounts debited and credited. The animation shows the answer. The prediction is marked and recorded as an attempt with XP. A wrong one waits in Mistakes as the question "What does this transaction do to the accounting equation?".
- **Free play.** Build a transaction from plain-language events, see its entry first, then play it. You can also start a new business, go back to the running shop, or play a ready story.
- **Find the mistake.** A transaction is recorded wrongly and played. You name the error by its class, as the Rectification of Errors topic does (omission, commission or principle), and by its effect: a one-sided error (a posting left out, a wrong amount or a wrong side in one account) tips the equation, and Suspense then takes the difference; a two-sided one leaves the equation standing with a wrong figure in it. Then the rectifying entry plays, through Suspense only where Suspense holds a difference, and the books are checked against what the right entry would have made.

The accounting-equation topic has two levels of the type `simulation`, placed right after its lesson: "See the Equation Move" (watch, then predict) and "Find the Mistake". Like any other level, each is passed by its share of right answers.

## How it is built

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| `src/simulator/engine.ts` | Turns books (ledger balances) and a transaction (journal lines) into steps. A step is one movement from the account credited to the account debited, or outside the business. The engine checks the equation after every step. It is pure and unit-tested. |
| `src/simulator/scenes.ts` | **The plug-in shape.** Every topic-specific part is here, and the engine and the screen know nothing of any topic. |
| `src/simulator/equation/` | The accounting-equation scene: its bands, opening books, starter list, stories, free-play events, mistakes, and the converters from the topic generators. |
| `src/simulator/explain.ts`, `predict.ts`, `questions.ts` | The words of every step and explanation, the marking of a prediction, and the questions behind what is recorded. |
| `src/simulator/ids.ts`, `questionSource.ts` | The ids of recorded answers, `sim:<scene>:<item>`, and how each one is turned back into its question, for Mistakes. |
| `src/components/simulator/` | The 2.5D renderer (`Tanks.tsx`: HTML boxes moved by transform and opacity only; the balance under them is a small SVG), the stage and its controls, and the four modes. |

## Adding a scene

A scene is a `Scene` object (see `src/simulator/scenes.ts`):

1. **`containers`**: what is drawn. Each container has a title, a caption, a side of the equation (`left`, `right`, or `either` for a temporary account such as Suspense, which stands on the side of its balance) and a hue. Use `transient` for a container that appears only while it holds something, such as Suspense or a Provision for Depreciation.
2. **`bands`**: how accounts are placed. The first band whose `takes(accountId, group)` accepts an account is where that account goes. `group` is the account's group from the engine's chart: asset, contra-asset, liability, capital, income, expense, drawings or temporary. End with catch-all bands, so that any entry can be placed. Mark `mayBeNegative` on a band that may fall below nothing (a loss, drawings, a provision) and `deduction` on one shown as "Less: ...".
3. **`equation`**: the containers on the left and on the right of the line under the tanks.
4. **`opening`**: the books a play starts from, as an opening entry.
5. **What it plays**: `transactions` (its own list, each with the entry, a why, and traps: wrong effects a student is likely to give), `generators` (endless transactions from a topic's templates, loaded on demand through `content/loadTopics.ts`), `stories`, `events` for free play, and `mistakes` (scenarios that make a wrongly recorded transaction, the question about it and the rectifying entry).

Then:

- Add the scene to `SCENES` in `scenes.ts`, and add its id and topic to `SCENE_TOPICS` in `ids.ts`.
- Write its unit tests in the style of `src/simulator/engine.test.ts`: every transaction balances after every step, the effects match the entry, and every mistake's correction restores the books.
- To put it on a topic's ladder, add `simulations: [{ title, description, sceneId, mode: 'predict' | 'mistakes', count, watch? }]` to the topic's `buildLadder` spec. The levels stand right after the lesson and take ids of their own (`<topic>-simulation-<n>`), so no other level is renumbered and no level's questions move.

A scene is data and functions. The renderer reads only containers, bands and steps, so another renderer (3D, or a printed worksheet) could draw the same scene.

## Labs

The simulator is a shelf of labs (`/simulator?lab=<id>`), each the picture that explains its part of the course best. The tanks suit the accounting equation; other topics have their own pictures: postings filling T-accounts, a book value falling year by year, a cash book and a pass book coming to agree, a trial balance on a scale, balances sorted into the final accounts, a profit shared among partners, a share through its calls, the flows of cash, ratios on dials.

- `src/simulator/labs.ts` lists every lab: its id, title, one-line blurb, search keywords, class, topics, and whether it is `ready`. A lab that is not ready is offered nowhere.
- `src/components/simulator/labs/index.ts` maps each id to its screen, loaded with `next/dynamic` only when the lab is opened. A screen takes `LabProps` (the `?seed=` of the address).
- The shelf puts the labs of the learner's own course first, and marks the others with their class.
- The logic of a lab (the figures, the accounting, the steps) sits in `src/simulator/<lab>/` as pure, unit-tested functions; the screen only draws it.

### The kit

Every lab is built from one kit, `src/components/simulator/labs/kit/`, so that the fifteen look and work alike. A lab's own folder (`labs/<lab>/`) holds only its pictures and the panels made of them.

- **The shell** (`LabShell.tsx`): `LabShell` lays out every lab the same way under the heading the simulator gives it: the mode switch, the conventions the lab follows (a `Conventions` panel, folded away until opened, one item each), any setting that holds in every mode, and the panel of the mode chosen (`<prefix>-panel-<mode>`). `Intro` says what the example is (a line or two, or its facts; `<prefix>-about`), and `Card` frames a part of the picture with its title.
- **The controls** (`LabControls.tsx`): `ModeSwitch` is Watch / Play / Try it as pills, the look of the equation lab's own modes (`<prefix>-mode-<mode>`); `Toggle` is a row of pills for an example, a method or a kind of question (`<prefix>-<value>`); `SelectField` is a labelled list for more examples than fit in a row (`<prefix>-example` where it picks the example).
- **The playback** (`usePlayback.ts`, `Playback.tsx`): `usePlayback(count, { story, stepMs, start, floor, run })` keeps how many steps are shown, plays and pauses them, steps on and back, replays, seeks, and has a speed of 0.5x, 1x or 2x. A step may be animated (`run`): it counts as shown when it has landed. A new story starts again; `start` past the end shows everything at once (the sandbox), `floor` keeps steps done by hand. `PlaybackBar` is Play / Pause, Back, Step, Replay, "Step n of N" and the speed, with the polite announcement of the step just played (`-play`, `-pause`, `-back`, `-step`, `-replay`, `-speed-<s>`, `-progress`, `-announce`). `Stage` frames the picture; with a playback it takes the keys when it has the focus: Space plays or pauses, the right and left arrows step on and back, R replays (`-stage`, with `data-shown` and `data-status`). `StepNow` tells the step just played ("Step 3 of 8: title", then what happened; before the first, how to start; `-now`), and `StepList` lists every step, each a button that goes to it (`-steps`, `-step-text`).
- **Try it** (`TryIt.tsx`, `TryItCard.tsx`, `QuestionRound.tsx`, `answers.ts`): `TryCard` is the one card: what the question is about, the score of the visit ("2 right of 3 (not recorded)"), the facts, the question, the answer, Check (or Skip) and Next, and `FeedbackBox`: ✓ Right or ✗ Not quite, the right answer, the slip named, the reason (`-try`, `-try-score`, `-try-prompt`, `-try-item`, `-try-check`, `-try-next`, `-try-feedback` with `data-correct`). Its parts are `ChoiceButton` (an option: pressed with a ring, marked ✓ or ✗ once checked, `data-mark`), `ChoiceGroup` and `AnswerField` (a figure: ₹ before an amount, % after a percentage, ": 1" or "times" after a ratio; Enter checks; a figure that cannot be read is said so under it). `TryItCard` asks a question of `answers.ts`: an amount in rupees, a percentage, a ratio (right in any terms that reduce to it), with the word that goes with it where one is asked, one option or every option that applies (`-try-answer`, `-try-option-<id>`, `-try-tag-<id>`); a lab with its own marking passes `judge`. `QuestionRound` asks the rounds of `src/simulator/tryit.ts` (partnership, shares). A lab whose answers are given on its picture (a side of a T-account, a pan of the scale, a place in the final accounts) builds its own question from the same parts.
- **The rest**: `MoneyField`, `NumberSlider`, `NumberField` and `Slider` (`MoneyField.tsx`) for figures in Play; `TAccount` and `Journal` (`TAccount.tsx`), an account and journal entries as the board writes them; `Arrive` and `Leave` (`Arrive.tsx`), something coming into or going out of a picture; `flyChip`, `fly` and `useFlights` (`flights.ts`), an amount flying from where the value comes from to where it goes; `amountText` and `useVisitSeed` (`format.ts`).
- **Accessibility**: every control is at least 44 px tall and shows its focus; a pressed control is filled (a chosen option ringed), a marked one carries ✓ or ✗, so colour is never the only signal; with reduced motion nothing flies and the pictures fade; the steps are told in words and announced; nothing scrolls sideways at 360 px; every lab can be used from the keyboard.

### Journal to ledger to trial balance (`posting`)

The picture: the journal of a small business for April 2026 on the left (above, on a phone), its ledger as T-accounts on the right, and the trial balance under them. Each line of an entry lifts off the journal and flies to its account: a debit to the debit side with "To", a credit to the credit side with "By", each naming the other account of the entry, and the L.F. of the journal line is filled in with the account's page. Then each account is balanced on 30 April: "Balance c/d" on the shorter side, the two sides totalled to the same figure, and the balance flies down to the other side as "Balance b/d" on 1 May. Last, each balance drops into the debit or credit column of the trial balance, and the two totals agree. Every posting carries its reason, by the rules of debit and credit for the kind of account (asset, liability, capital, income, expense), as NCERT gives them.

- **Watch**: five ready months (a first month; a customer on credit, with a bank account and a return; a supplier paid in full, whose account has no balance and is left out; drawings and a commission; a debt settled at a discount, a compound entry whose account shows one item for each account on the other side). The kit's playback (see "The kit").
- **Play**: start from a ready month, new figures (`?seed=` starts them) or an empty journal; add transactions of sixteen kinds with an amount (each dated after the last entry, so the journal stays in the order of dates, up to 29 April), remove the last, and switch the balancing and the trial balance on or off. A transaction that cannot happen (more cash paid out than is in hand, a party paid more than it is owed) is refused with the reason.
- **Try it**: which side of which account a journal line lands on and what its particulars say (tapping a side of a T-account), the balance c/d of an account and the side it is written on, and the column of the trial balance an account goes in (or none).
- **The board's format**: the journal (Date, Particulars, L.F., Dr, Cr, the narration), the trial balance (Name of account, L.F., Debit, Credit), and any account written in full with Date, Particulars, J.F. and Amount on each side.
- Conventions stated on the screen: amounts in rupees; the modern rules of debit and credit (by the kind of account); the trial balance by the balance method only, an account with no balance left out; the accounts of incomes and expenses are balanced only to carry their balances to the trial balance (at the year end they are closed to the Trading and Profit and Loss Account).
- Logic: `src/simulator/posting/` (`story.ts` the stories, the transactions and the reasons; `ledger.ts` the posting, the balancing, the trial balance and the steps; `tryit.ts` the questions). Screen: `src/components/simulator/labs/PostingLab.tsx` and `labs/posting/` (`Stage`, `parts`: the journal, the T-accounts and the trial balance, which the scale lab uses too).

### The trial balance scale (`trial-balance-scale`)

The picture: a balance scale whose debit pan holds the debit balances of the trial balance and whose credit pan holds the credit balances, each a labelled weight. An error card is dropped on the books: the weights it touches change, and the beam tips toward the heavier pan, or stays level when the error leaves the totals equal. When the trial balance is prepared, a hatched Suspense weight goes on the lighter pan and levels it; the rectifying entry puts the weights back and Suspense leaves the pan. Eight cards: a complete omission, a partial omission, a wrong account, a wrong amount in one account, a wrong side, a wrong total carried forward, an error of principle and compensating errors.

- **Watch**: any card on the books of Kiran Traders (a trial balance of ₹2,80,000), found after the trial balance (the error, Suspense, the rectification) or before it (the error, the correction, a trial balance that agrees).
- **Play**: the card, its figures (the amount, and the wrong figure where there is one), when it is found, and the stage, with new books from a seed. Figures that do not suit the books are refused with the reason.
- **Try it**: will this error tip the scale and which way; which pan Suspense goes on and for how much; the rectifying entry after the trial balance (with three wrong entries, each a named slip); the class of the error.
- **The board's format**: the trial balance as it stands (Suspense A/c in it), the rectifying entry found before and found after the trial balance, and Suspense A/c opened with "Difference in Trial Balance" and closed by the rectifying entry.
- Conventions (stated on the screen): the difference goes to Suspense A/c on the shorter side; a one-sided error found before the trial balance is corrected in the account itself, with no journal entry; found after, it is rectified through Suspense A/c; a two-sided error is rectified between the accounts concerned, never through Suspense (a complete omission by passing the entry that was left out). Compensating errors of casting (Sales A/c and Rent A/c overcast alike) found before the trial balance are each corrected in their own account, with no journal entry, since each is a wrong total in one account; found after, the two corrections make one entry between the two accounts, as the topic teaches. Every error is written as what was posted and what should have been, as in the topic's generators, so its effect, its Suspense and its entry follow from those two lists.
- Logic: `src/simulator/trial-balance-scale/` (`scale.ts`, `tryit.ts`). Screen: `src/components/simulator/labs/TrialBalanceScaleLab.tsx` and `labs/trial-balance-scale/`.
### When the trial balance won't agree (`trial-balance-fix`)

Class 11, topics `rectification-of-errors`, `trial-balance` and `ledger`. Where the scale lab drops one error card on a finished trial balance, this lab walks a whole month of a business from its books to a trial balance that will not agree, and back. The picture is a desk: the month's transactions and the books as the clerk wrote them (the journal, and in some cases a Sales or Purchases Book) on the left, the ledger as rough work on the right (a row of accounts, each with its balance and its marks; any one opens as a T-account in the board's format with Date, Particulars, J.F. and amount), and below them the trial balance beside Suspense A/c. The L.F. column of the journal fills in as each line is posted, and stays blank where a line never was. Errors are marked where they live once found: ✗ on a wrong posting, a dashed row for a posting that was never made, ✗ on a total that was added up wrongly (the rows above it visibly do not add up to it), ✎ on a correction made in the account itself (the wrong figure struck out). The Suspense board shows Suspense A/c in full, a bar of what is left in it, and each error found with its share in the difference (one-sided) or "not in Suspense A/c" (two-sided). The rectifying entries are posted with their error's letter in the date column; each account carries on from its balance brought down and is balanced again; Suspense A/c closes with nothing left; the corrected trial balance agrees, and the profit as per the books is corrected error by error.

- **Watch:** four ready cases of rising difficulty, each played step by step (the books, posting, balancing, the trial balance, checking it, Suspense A/c, each error with its entry and why, posting the rectifications, Suspense closing, the corrected trial balance, the profit). Asha Stationers (a new business; an error of principle, a partial omission and a wrong amount: Suspense ₹11,600 Dr; profit ₹7,100 corrected to ₹15,500); Kiran Traders (a Sales Book undercast, a wrong side, a wrong account, a complete omission, and a posting left out of Cash A/c that is found before the trial balance and corrected in the account: Suspense ₹21,000 Cr); Meera Electricals (opening stock, a Purchases Book posting for a wrong amount, a machine debited to Purchases, compensating castings of Sales A/c and Wages A/c, Cash A/c carried down at ₹65,600 for ₹66,500, and Deepa's balance in the credit column of the trial balance, found when it is checked; the books show a loss of ₹5,500, corrected to a profit of ₹19,500); Farhan Book House (six errors, for the detective). Each error's card says how it would have been put right had it been found before the trial balance.
- **Play:** the student takes a case (a ready one, or a new case from the seed) through six stages: the books; the ledger posted and balanced (a posting found wrong before the trial balance is told and corrected in its account); the trial balance, checked against the ledger before the student puts the difference to Suspense A/c (amount and column, with named slips and "Show me"); the errors, told as the board gives them or found by the detective, who ticks every posting of the journal and the book against its account and adds up every account again (a tick or a cross lands on each; "Show me", "Tick the rest for me"; an error of principle and a complete omission cannot be found by ticking and are told as found later from the vouchers); the rectifying entry of each error (debit account, credit account, amount and narration, each wrong entry told by its slip: the wrong way round, Suspense used for a two-sided error, Suspense missing for a one-sided one, an account that was right, half or the whole of a wrong-side or wrong-amount posting, the narration of another error); and the corrected books.
- **Try it:** will this error upset the trial balance, and which column comes out larger; how much went to Suspense A/c and in which column, from the errors found later (slips: adding the effects regardless of direction, a wrong side counted once, counting two-sided errors); the rectifying entry (three wrong entries, each a named slip); the corrected balance of an account after one error (with debit or credit); and how an error is put right when it is found before the trial balance. Each question stands on the ledger of its case, which moves on to the answer once it is answered.
- **Conventions** (stated on the screen), from `src/content/cbse11/{ledger,trial-balance,rectification-of-errors}.ts` and the topic's generators: one month, April 2026, balanced on 30 April and brought down on 1 May; credit sales (purchases) of goods in the Sales (Purchases) Book, each party posted on its day and the total on 30 April as "By (To) Sundries as per Sales (Purchases) Book"; the trial balance by the balance method only; before Suspense A/c is opened the trial balance is checked against the ledger, and a balance left out or in the wrong column is put right in the trial balance itself with no journal entry (a slip in drawing it up, not in the books); the rest of the difference goes to Suspense A/c in the shorter column as "Difference in Trial Balance"; a one-sided error found after the trial balance is rectified through Suspense A/c, found before it in the account itself with no entry; a two-sided error (complete omission, principle, wrong account, compensating errors) by an entry between the accounts, never through Suspense (compensating castings make one entry between the two accounts after the trial balance; found before it, each is corrected in its own account, as in the scale lab); a wrong side by twice the amount, a wrong amount by the difference; rectifying entries posted with their error's letter in the date column; the profit corrected for the listed errors only (no depreciation on an asset wrongly debited to Purchases). Every error is written as what was recorded and what should have been: the recorded books and the true books are both built, and each error's effect on every account (`delta`), on the trial balance, its rectifying entry and its effect on the profit follow from them; the tests check that the recorded books corrected by the entries are the true books.
- Logic: `src/simulator/trial-balance-fix/` (`model.ts` the books as recorded and as they should be, the ledger drawn at each stage, the trial balance, Suspense A/c and the errors worked out; `cases.ts` the four ready cases and a month for any seed; `walk.ts` the steps of Watch; `marking.ts` the marking of an entry, the wrong entries offered and the detective's checks; `tryit.ts` the questions). Tests: every ready case worked by hand, and 120 seeded cases whose books balance after rectification with Suspense A/c closed. Screen: `components/simulator/labs/TrialBalanceFixLab.tsx` and `labs/trial-balance-fix/` (`LedgerAccount` an account with its marks, `Books`, `Boards`, `Desk`, `PlayPanel`). Test ids begin `lab-trial-balance-fix-`; browser spec `e2e/lab-trial-balance-fix.spec.ts`.

### Where the cash came from and went (`cash-flow`)

A reservoir of cash and cash equivalents fed and drained by three pipes, operating, investing and financing, and beside it the Cash Flow Statement in the board's format (AS 3 Revised, indirect method, a company that is not a financing company), drawn as a waterfall: each figure is a bar that steps from where the running figure of its part stood, right for an item added or an inflow, left (hatched) for an item deducted or an outflow, and each sub-total is a bar from nil. So the operating part shows the "filters" from net profit before tax to cash: non-cash and non-operating items, then working capital changes, then tax paid. A pipe empties into the reservoir (or drains it) when its part's net is written, and the reservoir ends at the closing cash, which is the check on the statement.

- **Watch** writes a ready year row by row (play, pause, back, step, replay, speed), each step told with its reason and announced.
- **Play** has three ready years (a growing year; a loss year with cash from operations; profit up, cash down) and made-up years from the seed, and every figure on a slider: the profit, depreciation, goodwill written off, a machine sold (book value and price), interest paid, interest and dividend received, five working capital balances at both dates, tax paid, machinery and investments bought, shares and debentures issued, a loan repaid, the change in bank overdraft, dividend paid and the opening cash. A closing cash below nil is said in words.
- **Try it**: under which activity an item goes (or none), whether an item is added or deducted on the way from profit to cash (or not adjusted there), and a figure of the statement (operating profit before working capital changes, cash generated from operations, net cash from operating activities, closing cash) worked out with its rows hidden.
- Conventions: interest and dividend received are investing inflows and interest paid a financing outflow; bank overdraft is a short-term borrowing (financing), not deducted from cash, as the topic and CBSE answers take it; each year gives the net profit before tax directly (the screen says that from a change in Surplus the provision for tax, the dividend paid and the transfer to reserves are first added back); the tax actually paid comes after cash generated from operations; goodwill written off is added back; rows of nil are left out.
- Logic in `src/simulator/cash-flow/` (`model.ts`: the statement and its rows; `examples.ts`; `steps.ts`; `tryit.ts`), screen in `components/simulator/labs/CashFlowLab.tsx` and `labs/cash-flow/`. Test ids start `lab-cash-flow-`; browser spec `e2e/lab-cash-flow.spec.ts`.

### Ratios on the dials (`ratios`)

A balance sheet and a statement of profit and loss in brief, with the sub-totals the ratios use, and the fifteen ratios of the syllabus on dials in their four groups: liquidity (current, quick), solvency (debt to equity, total assets to debt, proprietary, interest coverage), activity (inventory, trade receivables, trade payables and working capital turnover) and profitability (gross profit, operating, operating profit and net profit ratios, return on investment). A needle swings when a figure changes; each dial gives its value, its change after a transaction (▲, ▼ or "no change") and the figures plugged in. A dial in focus is shown large with its formula, what makes each term, and what it means, and the lines of its terms are marked TOP and BOTTOM in the statements.

- **Watch** works out every dial in turn on a ready company, then plays three transactions (paying trade payables, buying goods on credit, issuing debentures) and says why each needle moved, with the rule that the same amount taken from both terms raises a ratio above 1 : 1.
- **Play** has three ready companies (steady; current ratio below 1 : 1 and negative working capital; heavy debt and a loss with a debit balance in reserves) and made-up ones from the seed, every figure on a slider, and fifteen transactions to play and undo. In the balance sheet, cash and cash equivalents takes up every change so that it still balances (and a change goes no further than the cash there is); the statement of profit and loss does not re-cast the balance sheet.
- **Try it**: will a transaction increase, decrease or not change a ratio (worked out by exact arithmetic on the figures on screen, then shown on the dials), a ratio worked out from the statements to two decimal places (common slips are named), and where an item goes (quick asset, current asset but not quick, current liability, debt, shareholders' funds, non-current asset).
- Conventions, from `src/content/cbse12/accounting-ratios.ts`: quick assets = current assets − inventories − prepaid expenses; debt = long-term borrowings + long-term provisions; equity = shareholders' funds; proprietary ratio on total assets; capital employed = shareholders' funds + non-current liabilities; turnover ratios on the average of the opening and closing balances, except working capital turnover on the closing working capital; net credit purchases = cost of revenue from operations + closing − opening inventories − purchases for cash (no direct expenses; every other purchase on credit, so goods bought for cash in Play leave the trade payables turnover alone); the net profit ratio on the profit after tax; tax at the rate given on a profit before tax, none on a loss. In a transaction, a profit or loss goes to reserves and surplus in full, tax on it ignored, as the board's questions take it. A ratio is an exact fraction, rounded half away from nil to two decimal places only when shown; one that cannot be worked out (no debt, negative working capital) says why.
- Logic in `src/simulator/ratios/` (`model.ts`, `deals.ts`, `examples.ts`, `steps.ts`, `tryit.ts`), screen in `components/simulator/labs/RatiosLab.tsx` and `labs/ratios/`. Test ids start `lab-ratios-`; browser spec `e2e/lab-ratios.spec.ts`.
### Sharing among partners (`partnership`)

The year's profit as a pool emptied into the partners, and the profit-sharing ratio as a circle whose wedges change hands. Logic in `src/simulator/partnership/` (exact fractions in `fraction.ts`, a split to the paisa in `split.ts`, `appropriation.ts`, `reconstitution.ts`, ready examples, Try-it questions); screen in `PartnershipLab.tsx` and `labs/partnership/`.

- **The profit.** The net profit pours into the Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c; interest on drawings runs back into it from the partners; interest on capital, salaries and commission are drawn off to the partners; what is left splits in the ratio; a guarantee moves the deficiency from the partners who bear it. Each partner is a column that fills with what is credited and dips below the line (hatched) for what is debited. The appropriation account builds in the board's format beside it, then the partners' capital (and, with fixed capitals, current) accounts in columns.
- **The ratio.** Admission, a new ratio, retirement or death: the wedges given up come loose (the sacrifice), then turn round the centre into their new owner's place (the new ratio), keeping their old owner's colour with the new owner's outline. A table of old share, change and new share gives the sacrificing or gaining ratio; goodwill then flies between the capital accounts and the journal entries are written.
- **Conventions an examiner should check.** Interest on capital, salary and commission are appropriations: with a loss none is allowed; when the profit (with interest on drawings) falls short of them, the whole of it is shared in proportion to the amounts due (the deed giving none of them priority; the app's rule for interest on capital, NCERT, extended to salary and commission). Interest on capital is on the opening capital for the year; interest on drawings is six months on the total (dates not given); commission is on the net profit before charging it. A guarantee by the firm is borne by the other partners in their own ratio. For a change in partners, capitals fluctuate and no goodwill stands in the books; the premium brought in cash goes through Premium for Goodwill A/c to the sacrificing partners; a premium not brought is debited to the new partner's capital account; on retirement or death the leaving partner is credited with that share of goodwill (and a remaining partner whose share falls is credited with goodwill on the sacrifice) and the gaining partners debited in the gaining ratio (AS 26). New figures are always in whole rupees (every share, interest and goodwill), as a board question has them.
- **Modes.** Watch (five profit examples, five changes in partners), Play (every figure of the firm and of the change, ready examples, new figures from the seed), Try it (a partner's share, interest when the profit falls short, a guarantee, a new, sacrificing or gaining ratio, who is credited with goodwill). ### Depreciation over the years (`depreciation`)

The asset account is a bar as wide as the cost. Each year end a slice of depreciation is cut from the book value and drops into a tray, "Charged to Profit and Loss A/c": an expense of the year, though no cash moves. - **Conventions** (stated on the screen): the year ends on 31 March; an asset is bought and sold on the first of a month; depreciation for the whole months of use; each charge to the nearest rupee (under the straight line method the rounded charge is the same every full year, and the year in which the life runs out takes what is left above the scrap value); a sale is received by cheque. ### Cash book meets pass book (`bank-reconciliation`)

- **Conventions** (stated on the screen), from the app's topics: the Cash Book has cash and bank columns only, no discount column; a cash discount is recorded in the Journal Proper (Discount Allowed A/c Dr, To the debtor; the creditor Dr, To Discount Received A/c) and the Cash Book shows the money alone; a cheque received is banked the same day; cash paid into or drawn from the bank for office use is a contra entry, marked "C" and not posted; the goods books take credit dealings in the goods of the business only, at the price after trade discount (never recorded); an asset bought on credit, goods taken by the proprietor (at cost, GST ignored) and a bad debt go to the Journal Proper; GST on the price after trade discount, CGST and SGST at half the rate each within the state, IGST across states, at 5% or 18%; each party posted with its invoice or note on the day (a row for each account of the entry), the column totals posted at the month end as "To (or By) Sundries as per" the book; a cash dealing with GST has a Cash Book row for the goods and for each tax; petty cash on the imprest system, reimbursed on the last day with exactly what was spent, its analysis columns posted as totals (each head Dr, To Petty Cash A/c). The ledger accounts are shown with the month's postings in the order of their dates, not balanced.
- Logic: `src/simulator/books-of-entry/` (`model.ts` the records, the books, the postings and the ledger, built on `engine/books.ts`; `examples.ts`; `walk.ts`; `tryit.ts`). Screen: `components/simulator/labs/BooksOfEntryLab.tsx` and `labs/books-of-entry/` (`Desk`, `BookViews`, `Posting`, `Stage`, `PlayPanel`). Test ids begin `lab-books-of-entry-`; browser spec `e2e/lab-books-of-entry.spec.ts`.

### Profit without full books (`incomplete-records`)

Class 11, topic `incomplete-records`, by the statement of affairs method only (the conversion method is outside the syllabus). The picture is two statements of affairs side by side, on 1 April and on 31 March, each a pair of stacks on one scale: the blocks of what the business owns against the blocks of what it owes, with a dashed gap that the capital fills as the balancing block, so the two stacks stand level. The proprietor's private flat, car or housing loan is shown crossed out, "left out, not the business's". Then the capital ladder: the closing capital, the drawings added back, the additional capital taken off (hatched), the adjusted closing capital, and the opening capital beside it; the gap between the two is the profit (or the loss). Then each adjustment steps the profit found to the net result, a deduction hatched. Beside it the board's formats: the Statements of Affairs in two columns and the Statement of Profit or Loss, each figure appearing when the picture reaches it.

- **Watch:** Menon Hardware (a profit: cash drawn monthly and goods taken at cost, capital brought in, interest on capital, depreciation, a provision for doubtful debts and a prepaid expense: net profit ₹40,800) and Sheikh Tailors (a loss: a salary taken as drawings, a private bill paid by the business, an overdraft at the end, depreciation, wages outstanding and commission accrued: net loss ₹18,500).
- **Play:** every item on both dates, the drawings (a month or for the year, goods at cost with their sale price told, a private bill), the additional capital and each adjustment; three ready examples (the third: the capital falls by ₹40,000, yet the year made a profit of ₹50,000) and new figures from the seed. Figures that leave no capital are refused with the reason.
- **Try it:** the capital on a date (slips named: liabilities added, a private item taken in, an overdraft as an asset), the profit or loss before adjustments with the word (slips: the change in capital alone, drawings deducted, additional capital added, goods at sale price, one month's drawings), the net result after the adjustments (each sign turned round, interest on the closing capital), and where an item goes (assets side, liabilities side, left out, drawings, additional capital, deducted from or added to the profit found).
- **Conventions** (stated on the screen), from `src/content/cbse11/incomplete-records.ts` and its templates: capital = business assets − business liabilities; profit = closing capital + drawings − additional capital − opening capital; goods taken at cost; a salary the proprietor takes, and a private bill paid by the business, are drawings; the closing statement is drawn up before the adjustments, which are then made to the profit found (interest on capital on the capital at the beginning of the year; depreciation on the figure in the closing statement; the provision on the closing debtors; outstanding expenses deducted; prepaid expenses and accrued incomes added). Amounts worked out by a rate are rounded to the nearest rupee.
- Logic: `src/simulator/incomplete-records/` (`model.ts`, `examples.ts`, `walk.ts`, `tryit.ts`). Screen: `components/simulator/labs/IncompleteRecordsLab.tsx` and `labs/incomplete-records/` (`Picture`, `Statements`, `InputsPanel`). Test ids begin `lab-incomplete-records-`; browser spec `e2e/lab-incomplete-records.spec.ts`.

### Accounts in a spreadsheet (`spreadsheet`)

Class 12, topic `computerised-accounting` (the option a school may teach in place of Part B, Analysis of Financial Statements). The picture is a small spreadsheet that works: a grid with column letters and row numbers, each cell holding a number, text or a formula, and above it the name box and the formula bar. Every formula is worked out by the lab's own evaluator (no `eval`, no library), again whenever a cell it names changes. The cells a formula names are outlined in the colour of their reference, and the line under the formula bar reads the cell in words: its formula with each reference in that colour, what it gives and from which figures, or the error and its reason. A copy flies from the cell copied to each cell it lands in, carrying the formula it becomes there, and the card "What the copy did" sets each formula beside its copy, every reference marked as moved (relative), staying (absolute), mixed, or gone off the sheet (#REF!). A chart (pie or bars) is drawn as SVG from a range of names and a range of figures, the pie with a key naming each slice with its figure, share and angle.

- **Watch:** seven ready workbooks, the ones the syllabus names, each built step by step (play, pause, step, back, replay; Space, the arrows and R), every step told with the value it gives: a bank reconciliation statement in Plus and Minus columns (Kavya Stores: ₹48,000 in the cash book, ₹43,600 in the pass book; SUM copied across, then IF for the side of the balance); a straight line schedule (SLN with absolute references copied down, accumulated depreciation as `=SUM($B$6:B6)`, the book value down to the salvage value); a written down value schedule by DB (the rate by `=ROUND(1-(B2/B1)^(1/B3),3)`, 50%, then ₹80,000, ₹40,000, ₹20,000, ₹10,000); written down value with the rate in a cell (`=B5*$B$2`, 25% on ₹4,00,000); a loan repayment schedule (PMT ₹1,33,100 on ₹3,31,000 at 10% for 3 years; interest, principal and balance to nil; the totals); six ratios from a statement of profit and loss and a balance sheet (brackets where a term is worked out from more than one cell: the quick assets, the average inventory, the debt of long-term borrowings and long-term provisions; ROUND to two places); and the shares and angles of five heads of expense, then a pie chart and a bar chart of them. The grid can be walked with the arrows at any step, and a Values / Formulas switch shows the formulas in their cells.
- **Play:** a working sheet: any ready workbook (finished) or a blank one of 6 columns and 12 rows. Type into a cell (from the keyboard, or in the formula bar; Enter keeps it, Escape leaves it), select a block (Shift and an arrow, or Shift and a click), copy and paste, fill down or right, clear, undo (Control and C, V, D, R, Z; Delete); while a formula is typed, a cell clicked is named in it. Errors show in the cell (#DIV/0!, #NAME?, #REF!, #VALUE!, #NUM!) with the reason under the bar; a formula that cannot be read is refused with the reason (a bracket not closed, two values side by side, too few arguments). "New figures" (seeded by `?seed=`) puts new figures in the given cells of a ready workbook, chosen as the topic's own questions choose them, so that depreciation and loan figures come out in whole rupees. A chart of any range, pie or bars, follows every change.
- **Try it:** what a formula gives (the order of working, a commission, IF with >=, SUM less a cell, SLN, DB, PMT, IPMT, a reconciliation, a ratio with ROUND, COUNT; each slip named); what a copied formula becomes (the topic's `=B2*$C$1` and `=$A3*B$2`, a growing range, a copy across, a copy up off the sheet to #REF!; slips: copied as typed, $ ignored or read the wrong way round, rows or columns only, one reference only); which formula gives a figure (quick ratio, inventory turnover, gross profit ratio, a pass book balance, PMT, SLN, an angle or a depreciation to be copied down; each wrong formula says what it would give, worked out on the sheet); and spot the wrong reference (a copied formula whose damage shows on the sheet: a rate, an instalment or a total left relative, a year fixed with $; tap the reference). The cell asked about shows "?" until the answer, then the sheet shows the answer.
- **Conventions** (stated on the screen), the topic's own (`src/content/cbse12/computerised-accounting.ts` and its templates) and the spreadsheet's: a cell is named column then row; the order of working is Excel's and LibreOffice's (brackets, negation, %, ^, * and /, + and -, &, comparisons; the same rank left to right, so `-2^2` is 4 and `2^3^2` is 64); an empty cell is nil in arithmetic and "" in a comparison; a $ fixes the part right after it; a reference copied off the sheet becomes #REF!; a circular reference (a cell whose formula needs its own value) shows #REF! with a reason that names it a circular reference (Excel warns of a circular reference and shows 0; Google Sheets shows #REF!); `0^0` is #NUM!, as in Excel; paste copies what is typed, and a cell with a format of its own keeps it. Functions: SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNT, ROUND (half away from nil), IF (only the branch taken is worked out); SLN(cost, salvage, life) = (cost - salvage) ÷ life; DB(cost, salvage, life, period[, month]) as Excel works it out: rate = 1 - (salvage ÷ cost)^(1 ÷ life) rounded to three decimal places, charged on the book value at the start of each period (a first part year, and a part year after the life, when `month` < 12); PMT(rate, n, pv[, fv, type]) the payment at the end of each period, the loan entered as -B1 so that it comes out positive; IPMT and PPMT the interest on the balance at the start of the period and the rest of the instalment. Figures are kept in full and shown in the cell's format (₹ with the Indian grouping, to the paisa when not whole). In the workbooks the years end on 31 March; the reconciliation starts from the debit balance of the cash book; the ratios follow Accounting Ratios (quick assets = current assets - inventories - prepaid expenses; inventory turnover on the average inventory; net profit ratio on the profit after tax; debt to equity = (long-term borrowings + long-term provisions) ÷ shareholders' funds, as the topic defines debt); a slice's angle = figure ÷ total × 360°.
- **The shelf and Part B:** the lab belongs to the course of a learner who chose Computerised Accounting as Part B in Settings, listed with the other Class 12 labs, and the labs of Analysis of Financial Statements (cash flow, ratios, the company's statements) then follow under "Class 12 · Analysis of Financial Statements option"; a learner of the usual Part B finds it under "Class 12 · Computerised Accounting option" (`shelfGroups` in `src/simulator/labs.ts`, from what `useCourse` says the learner meets).
- Logic: `src/simulator/spreadsheet/` (`address.ts` cells, references and their moves; `formula.ts` the tokens, the parser and copying; `evaluate.ts` the values, the errors and the functions; `sheet.ts` typing, working out, formats, paste and fill; `workbooks.ts` the ready workbooks, their steps and words, new figures; `chart.ts`; `tryit.ts`). Tests: the evaluator and the functions against known values (Excel's own DB and IPMT examples among them), reference shifting, every ready workbook worked by hand, and 60 seeds of new figures for each workbook, free of errors, the schedules closing. Screen: `components/simulator/labs/SpreadsheetLab.tsx` and `labs/spreadsheet/` (`Grid`, `Formula` for the formula bar, the reading and the copy card, `Chart`, `Watch`, `Play`, `TryIt`). Test ids begin `lab-spreadsheet-` (cells `lab-spreadsheet-cell-B7`, with `data-shown` and `data-formula`); browser spec `e2e/lab-spreadsheet.spec.ts`.

## Performance and accessibility

- The page loads only the simulator and the scene. The topic generators are fetched the first time a generator is used (measured with `npm run measure`).
- No more than six coins move at once. Bands move by `transform` and coins by `transform` and `opacity`. A page that is not being drawn does not stall a step.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion`, no coin flies and bands fade from one state to the next.
- Colour is never the only signal. Every band is named with its amount, a change has ▲ or ▼ and a sign, a deduction is hatched, and the equation says "Balanced" or "Not balanced" in words.
